A — BITS (“costs 8 bits” names the whole aesthetic)

The fantasy: the smallest piece the system is built from. The mark is the GameCard's stepped silhouette itself (F-02's signature cut, promoted to money) — struck in gold, with a blue foil slash riding the step diagonal, kin to the cards' foil sweep. Every price is a pun the app earns: an 8-BIT effect is 8-bit.

Risks: “Bits” has prior art as Twitch's tipping currency (different context, worth knowing); the slash must stay coarse at 10px or it reads as dirt. Strength: the deepest system tie of the three — the money is literally cut from the card.

B — PIXELS (the ◈ heir, cut from 4px blocks)

The fantasy: you're buying the stuff pictures are made of — effects, finishes and fonts are all, in the end, pixels on your card. The mark rebuilds the ◈ placeholder as a pixel-cut gem: stepped facets, light top-left, ink bottom-right, blue glint pixels + a blue-lit stair edge. Of the three it's the only one that inherits the diamond's silhouette — everything already shipped with ◈ reads as the same coin.

Risks: “pixels” can feel small/cheap as a unit name; PX is developer jargon (here, arguably a feature). Strength: visual continuity with every existing mock + the tightest chip in the set (8 PX).

C — SPARKS (the step motif, set in motion)

The fantasy: the jolt of making something — a gold spark climbing the card's own staircase, blue motion trails streaming off each step. Spending one ignites an effect; the daily +1 is a little inspiration arriving. It's the most kinetic mark of the three and the natural fit for the counter's tick moment (the trails are pre-drawn motion).

Risks: three small cubes + trails is the busiest geometry at 10px (the trails carry it, but it's the first to blur); “sparks” carries a faint energy/regen connotation (much weaker than VOLTS, since sparks are discrete things you collect). Strength: motion, voice, and the step motif without borrowing the card's whole silhouette.
