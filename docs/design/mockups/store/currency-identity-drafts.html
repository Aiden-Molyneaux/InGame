Customizer Currency — Identity Drafts ×3 (name + mark)

Three in-app identities for the single soft currency (spec term “Customizer currency”, ECON-01/02 — unchanged; this is its display face). Each take = a name, a mark, and the mark living in every slot the chrome owns: CurrencyCounter · PriceChip · the Add Game 1-◈ adopt chip · PackTile · LedgerRow (draft C's ledger form, per the gate ruling) + the ECON-09 negative counter. The winner replaces the ◈ placeholder app-wide; the in-app name ripples ECON microcopy at formalization (spec-owner batch). Mark stays brand.gold in chrome (gold = value, F-02). Amounts illustrative (OQ-002/OQ-011).

A — CREDITS (insert coin, but ours)

The fantasy: the device is an arcade machine, and this is its money — “INSERT CREDIT” is the most load-bearing word in arcade history. Zero learning curve: every user already knows what a credit is and that it's spendable (§1.1's conventional pole, applied to vocabulary). The mark is a milled token punched with the GameCard's stepped square — the coin is literally minted from the card system.

Risks: the least ownable of the three (“credits” is genericised across gaming); no material/craft tie to customization specifically. Strength: instant legibility at every size; the word carries the retro thesis on its own.

B — INK (you buy the material, not the menu)

The fantasy: cards are printed objects — the editor's reveal is already a “first print” ritual (OQ-040), so the currency becomes the shop's material: gold ink. Spending it on an effect feels like consuming real stuff, not clearing a paywall; packs become refills; the daily login is a slow drip. Strongest craft tie of the three — the whole economy speaks the card-making language.

Note: “FOIL” was considered for this slot and rejected — it collides with the foil finish type (COSM-01/CARD-12; a finish named CLASSIC FOIL costing “6 foil” is nonsense). INK has no collision. Risks: mass-noun chip grammar (“1 INK”) is slightly odd; ink implies printers more than arcades — it leans craft over retro.

C — VOLTS (the device runs on it)

The fantasy: currency as the handheld's power — the counter is a charge cell, the login bonus is a trickle charge, and going negative (ECON-09) reads as a drained cell. It's the most device-native identity: the mark belongs to the same family as the LED pips and the power light, and “8V” makes the smallest possible price chip.

Risks: energy currencies in games usually regenerate — “volts” may set an auto-refill expectation the economy doesn't honour (the +1 login bonus softens but doesn't erase this); spending charge on cosmetics is a half-step metaphor stretch. Strength: tightest visual integration with the shell's light language.
